### 2. How disputes work under the managed oracle
- **Event signature: unchanged.** DisputePrice keeps the OOv2 shape and name (UMA docs, explicit). Your existing OOv2 ABI on 0x2C03… will decode it. You already successfully decode ProposePrice and Settle from this contract, consistent with the events being identical.
- **Who may dispute: anyone.** The whitelist restricts *proposing* only. Disputing is permissionless, with a matching ~$750 bond. This is stated repeatedly by UMA (blog, docs) and follows from the MOOV2 code path (`disputePriceFor` inherited from OptimisticOracleV2).
- **Paths to a contested outcome without a "fresh" DisputePrice — these matter for your accounting:**
  - **First-dispute auto-reset:** the CTF adapter resets the question on first dispute (a new request goes out). DisputePrice *is* emitted, but the disputed round is superseded and the market resolves on the replacement round. The OOReporter audit confirms this design: "On a dispute, the reporter automatically opens a replacement request… that resolution settles only the superseded round and is disregarded by the reporter as stale."
  - **Admin/manual resolution:** the adapter's QuestionManuallyResolved (admin flag with a 1-hour safety period) and, in the newer stack, the Polymarket OracleAggregator's `resolveResult` administrator/arbitrator override can resolve a market without a fresh dispute.
  - **Permissioned settlement (RESOLVER_ROLE):** in MOOV2, settlement is permissioned; `Expired` is treated as `Proposed` so requests do not auto-finalize — the resolver settles.
  - **Rule clarifications:** RequestRulesUpdated on MOOV2 / Polymarket "Additional context" bulletins can change how voters interpret a market without a new dispute event.
- **Escalation target: still the DVM on Ethereum mainnet** (VotingV2 0x004395edb43EFca9885CEdad51EC9fAf93Bd34ac), reached via the Polygon→mainnet tunnel. The managed variant explicitly "send[s] disputes to UMA's Data Verification Mechanism (DVM)" (UMIP-189).

### 3. Is the collapse expected? Yes.
UMA designed MOOV2 specifically to cut disputes, and published the results: incorrect proposals −59%, total disputes −68% in the first month; whitelisted proposers 99.7% accurate vs 85.8% non-whitelisted. Because disputers must risk a matching $750 bond and whitelisted proposals are ~99.7% accurate, the economic incentive to dispute nearly vanished. Bloomberg (May 2026) reported "nearly 2,000 Polymarket financial contracts have been disputed" cumulatively, with "In April alone, 230 contracts that attracted more than $1 billion in trading" going through the process, "up from 79 contracts six months earlier" — i.e., disputes scale with high-dollar contentious markets, not with routine sports/crypto proposals. The implied system-wide dispute rate near 0.04% matches your ~0.036% upper bound and your "≥80× below 2024 baseline" finding. **This is working as designed.** The residual risk is not "wrong-contract" but "small-window": at roughly 7–8 disputes/day, an 11-hour window expects only ~3–4 disputes, so observing zero is at the low tail — surprising enough to verify, not enough to prove a bug.
