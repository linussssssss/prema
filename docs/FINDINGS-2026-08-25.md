# FINDINGS — what we measured, and how the numbers moved

Companion to `docs/BRIEF-product-evolution.md` (the state-of-the-company input to
the first consult) and `WHERETOGO.md` (that consult's output). This document
exists for something neither covers: **the trajectory of the measurements** —
what each number was, what it is now, and why it changed.

That trajectory matters more than any single figure, because the direction of
correction has been consistent and it is not the direction we expected.

---

## 1. The headline trajectory

Every serious correction we have made moved text lift **up** and stakes lift
**down**. The two are converging, and the gap is now about a third of what we
first reported.

| Stage | What changed | Best text rule (stratified vs `disputed`) | Stakes (top decile) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Initial pooled reading | none — pooled, unstratified | 20.69x | — |
| Stratified by category | Simpson's paradox exposed | 1.08x (within Politics) | — |
| Blinded human study | controls matched on month/category/volume | 1.46x | — |
| Full corpus, ~21x positives | backfill completed | 1.57x (`vague-source`) | 6.45x |
| **Neg-risk join fixed (ADR-0024)** | disputed set 2,089 → 3,317 markets | **1.93x** (`vague-source`) | **5.48x** |

Also at the current cut: `no-na-condition` **2.23x**, `status-verb-gap` 1.83x,
`hedge-words` 1.11x (but it fires on 57% of the corpus, so it cannot rank
anything).

**Read this carefully.** The 20.69x was composition and we were right to kill it.
But the 1.4–1.6x band we then settled on was measured on a dispute set that
systematically excluded negative-risk markets — a non-random exclusion along the
exact axis (oracle regime) that shifts dispute rates. Correcting it moved the
best rule to 1.93x. **The signal is not settled, and the corrections keep going
one way.**

## 2. What is definitely dead

**Predicting disputes from listing text as a product.** Even at 1.93x, against a
base rate that is now ~0.07%/month and falling, a text screen cannot rank a
watchlist. `hedge-words` fires on 57% of markets; `vague-source` on 9.5%. And
~30% of *non-disputed* markets carry visible listing-time ambiguity (blinded
study). Ambiguity is too common and disputes too rare for a binary screen to be
decision-useful.

This is a ceiling in the signal, not a model-quality problem. A better extractor
does not fix it.

## 3. What is dying: the dispute signal itself

UMA's ManagedOptimisticOracleV2 proposer whitelist (enforced 2025-09-05,
confirmed by our own data: 11 MOOv2 proposals in Aug 2025, 13,711 in Sep).

| Month | MOOv2 proposals | MOOv2 dispute rate |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2025-09 | 13,711 | 0.263% |
| 2025-12 | 33,780 | 0.367% |
| 2026-03 | 133,624 | 0.254% |
| 2026-06 | 244,805 | 0.100% |
| 2026-08 | 477,060 | **0.068%** |

Not a step down to a plateau — a **continuing decline**, while proposal volume
grew **35x**. OOv2 is now vestigial (1,140 proposals in Aug 2026 vs MOOv2's
477,060). Like-for-like against the pre-whitelist ~1.38%, the collapse is nearer
**20x** than the 8.4x pooled figure we first quoted.

Building the company on `disputed` means building on a shrinking observable.

## 4. What is growing: two signals the whitelist does not touch

This is the part that was not in the first consult, and it changes the picture.

### 4.1 Voids rose while disputes fell

A void (50/50 payout) is a resolution failure that requires **no challenger** —
so the whitelist cannot suppress it.

| Period | Void rate |
|---|---:|
| 2025 (monthly range) | 0.054% – 0.521% |
| 2026-04 (peak) | 4.257% |
| 2026-08 | 1.980% |

**August 2026: 325 disputes against 16,420 voids.** A 50x gap.

Stratified by category (pre- vs post-whitelist), the rise is *not* mechanical
crypto ties — crypto essentially never voids:

| Category | Pre-whitelist | Post-whitelist |
|---|---:|---:|
| Esports | 0.262% | **11.882%** |
| Sports | 0.651% | **3.225%** |
| Tennis | — | 2.513% |
| Politics | 0.030% | 0.125% |
| Bitcoin | 0.000% | **0.000%** |
| Crypto | 0.056% | **0.005%** |

Honest caveat: sports/esports voids look substantially like real-world
contingencies — cancellations, forfeits, tennis retirements — rather than pure
rules ambiguity. Though a well-written rule *specifies* the retirement case, so
it is partly a rules-quality signal. Text predicts voids weakly too (best
stratified rule 3.37x, but it fires on 57% of markets).

### 4.2 Rules change after listing — and it concentrates where judgement lives

**This is the strongest and newest finding.** See ADR-0025.

We had reported `rules_edited_after_listing = 0` and twice described it as
time-gated on a worker. That was wrong — it was a **data limitation**. Every
market has exactly one Gamma rules version because the ingest captured each
market once, so edits were invisible regardless of elapsed time.

The chain carries an independent second snapshot: all 2,166,514
`QuestionInitialized` events hold the ancillary data committed at listing —
100% populated, ~1,489 chars, immutable, timestamped. Comparing it against
Gamma's current text answers the question **retroactively, across the corpus**.

| | count | share |
|---|---:|---:|
| compared | 2,017,202 | |
| identical | 1,984,941 | 98.401% |
| **drifted** | **32,258** | **1.599%** |
| unparsed | 3 | 0.000% |

And it is nowhere near uniform — **~10-16x concentration** in judgement-heavy
categories against the 1.6% baseline:

| Category | Drift rate |
|---|---:|
| Business | 26.3% |
| Awards | 15.8% |
| Politics | 15.1% |
| NBA | 13.2% |
| Music | 12.4% |
| Movies | 9.2% |

Sports and crypto — the mechanically-resolved bulk — barely move. **Editing
clusters precisely where resolution requires discretion.** This is the first
signal in the project whose concentration matches the thing we care about rather
than fighting it.

Magnitude: 62% of edits change length <2%, 11% by 10-50%, 100 markets rewritten
by >50%, and 1,878 changed text at *identical* length (a substitution). Our
normalizer already strips whitespace-only differences, so every one is a real
character change.

**What drift does NOT yet establish** — and must not be claimed:

1. **Not that edits were material or adverse.** A typo fix counts.
2. **Not when the edit happened.** We compare listing-time text to *current*
   text, so we cannot date it. Whether an edit landed before trading, during, or
   after resolution is exactly what makes it a *retroactive rule change* — the
   litigation-relevant distinction. Dating it is buildable but not built.
3. **Not that drift predicts disputes or voids.** Unmeasured. "Politics drifts
   and Politics disputes" would reproduce the confound that cost us the 20x.

## 5. Methodological facts a reader should hold

- **Every number must be stratified.** Sports is 1.38M of 2.6M markets and almost
  never disputes, so any rule firing more on Politics inherits a gap for free.
  This trap has caught us twice.
- **The volume decile table was an artifact.** 30.7% of markets have *no* volume,
  and `nulls first` swept them into deciles 1–3. The apparent "contested falls as
  stakes rise" gradient was missing data. Corrected, `contested` has no volume
  gradient at all; `disputed` rises 0.020% → 0.514% monotonically.
- **Stakes is unusable at listing time.** `volume_usd` is *final* volume. Ranking
  listings by it is hindsight and breaks our own non-negotiable. Legitimate only
  as a running feature — volume-to-date — i.e. a watchlist, not a listing score.
- **Three hardcoded figures in our own analysis went stale** as the corpus grew.
  The report now derives them from the data it prints.
