## 0. The strategic frame: speed is the strategy

Three clocks are running, and all three reward being early:

1. **The credibility clock.** Verdict's product is ultimately a track record —
   timestamped, hashed, pre-resolution calls whose accuracy anyone can audit.
   A track record cannot be bought, backfilled, or faked (our own no-hindsight
   rules forbid it). Every week the record starts earlier is a week no
   competitor can ever recover. This is the only compounding asset in the
   business; everything else is replicable.
2. **The news clock.** Disputes are at record highs, traders are suing both
   major venues, the CFTC rulemaking is open, and Kalshi's CEO has publicly
   conceded rule disclosure is broken. This is a temporary attention regime.
   The reference source for "was this market wording broken?" will be chosen
   by the market *during* this drama window, not after it.
3. **The incumbent clock.** The realistic killer is an in-house build at
   Kalshi or Polymarket US. Their build starts the day the problem is big
   enough to staff — our defense is that by then, the public, neutral,
   cross-venue record already exists and their internal tool structurally
   can't match it (nobody trusts a venue to referee its own disputes).

**Doctrine:** ship the public record first, monetize second, polish last.
When any marketing task competes with the calibration page going live, the
page wins.

## 1. Positioning

**One-liner:** *Verdict is the neutral resolution layer for prediction
markets — it measures dispute risk in market wording before you trade, and
shows its own accuracy in public.*

**The category we are creating:** **resolution risk** as a first-class,
quantified metric — like spread, liquidity, and volatility. The win condition
is linguistic: traders saying "check the Verdict score" the way they say
"check the spread." Category creation is cheap here because nobody else sells
the metric and the pain is famous.

**What we are:** the referee's assistant. Evidence, precedent, probability.
**What we are never** (non-negotiables, also legally load-bearing): a market
operator, a position taker, a token issuer, a custodian, or an oracle that
*rules*. Every public output is "recommended outcome + confidence + evidence,"
never a verdict-as-fact. Neutrality is not a value statement; it is the
product spec — COI attestations, append-only hashed logs, published methods.

**Naming:** the score is the **Verdict Score** (0–100 probability the market
gets contested). The nightly list is the **Dispute Watchlist**. The public
accuracy page is the **Calibration Page**. Use these names consistently from
day one so the vocabulary spreads with the content.

**Anti-positioning:** we are not a pundit, not a drama account, not
"prediction market news." Every public artifact must be regenerable from the
dataset with hashes. The tone is a court reporter, not a take merchant —
that's also the differentiation: everyone else in the niche sells opinions.

## 2. Audiences, in order of leverage

| # | Audience | What they want | What they give us | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Traders & bot operators (Polymarket/Kalshi power users) | Not losing money to wording traps | Distribution, virality, first API users | Now |
| 2 | Market makers & funds | Risk input for quoting/sizing | First real revenue (API) | Month 2–4 |
| 3 | Venues (Polymarket US, Kalshi, new CFTC venues) | Fewer disputes, regulator-friendly process | The big contracts (listing gate, escalation desk) | Month 4+ |
| 4 | Oracle networks (UMA, Chainlink) | Someone to handle the subjective tail | Partnership, acquisition path | Relationship now, deals later |
| 5 | Regulators, journalists, academics | A citable, neutral data source | Credibility that money can't buy | Continuous, low-effort |

The order matters: traders create the public proof that makes MMs pay; MM
revenue and usage stats make venues take the meeting; venue adoption plus the
corpus makes oracle networks acquirers instead of competitors. Regulators and
journalists are a force multiplier on every stage and cost only citations.

## 3. The content engine (the dataset IS the marketing)

Rule: **we never write content; we render it.** Every artifact below is a
query against the dataset plus a template — which is why a solo founder can
sustain daily cadence. If a piece can't be regenerated by a script, we don't
publish it.

**Recurring artifacts (automate in this order):**

1. **Dispute post-mortems** ("Anatomy of a dispute"). For every famous
   resolution mess since 2024: the exact rule text, the linter flags *it
   would have raised at listing*, the on-chain timeline (propose → dispute →
   vote), who lost money, and what one-sentence rewrite would have prevented
   it. This is the highest-virality format in the niche — drama with
   receipts — and it demonstrates the product without selling. Target: 2/week
   from the backlog of escalated disputes.
2. **Dispute Watchlist** (daily, automated): riskiest open markets by Verdict
   Score, with the flagged clause quoted. Post: X thread + web page + (later)
   the free API endpoint. This is the habit-forming product.
3. **Pre-resolution calls** (the track-record builder): for contested
   markets, publish recommended outcome + confidence *before* the oracle
   resolves, content-hashed, timestamped. Right or wrong, the honesty is the
   brand — a public "we were wrong here" entry buys more trust than ten wins.
4. **Calibration Page** (the flagship): predicted contest-rates vs. realized,
   overall and by category, updated automatically, with the full methodology
   and the time split published. This page is the product demo, the sales
   deck, and the seed-round slide, all in one URL.
5. **Wording of the Week**: one ambiguous clause, one suggested rewrite.
   Cheap, shareable, teaches the market our vocabulary.
6. **Quarterly "State of Resolution Risk" report**: the citable PDF for
   journalists, academics, and the CFTC docket. This is how we become the
   source everyone links.

**Launch splash (one-time):** open-source the dataset (per-venue ToS
permitting) + a technical write-up of how it was built (the UMA/CTF indexing,
the composite label). Post to HN / r/ethereum / prediction-market Twitter.
Engineers are the amplifiers for data products, and the 2026 academic papers
on LLM market-resolution give us authors to engage directly — offer them the
dataset, get the citation.

## 4. Channels (ranked by cost-effectiveness for a solo founder)

1. **X/Twitter** — where 100% of the niche lives. The account is the company
   for the first 6 months. Post artifacts, not takes. Reply with data under
   every dispute-drama thread within the hour (speed of reply is the growth
   hack; have the post-mortem template ready to fire).
2. **The venue-adjacent communities** — Polymarket Discord, UMA Discourse,
   r/Polymarket: answer dispute questions with evidence, link the tool, never
   spam. UMA Discourse especially: the voters *are* an audience.
3. **Hacker News** — for the dataset/methodology launches (engineering
   credibility → hiring, investors).
4. **Forecasting communities** (Metaculus/Manifold circles, EA forecasting) —
   small but disproportionately loud on calibration; they will stress-test
   the Calibration Page and evangelize it if it's honest.
5. **Direct DMs** — the entire first API cohort (bot operators, MM desks) is
   maybe 200 identifiable accounts. This is a DM motion, not a funnel. Offer
   free keys personally.
6. **The CFTC docket** — file a public comment in the rulemaking citing our
   data (disputes by category, value at stake). A comment letter is free,
   citable forever, and instantly separates us from every anonymous crypto
   tool. Also: it's marketing regulators read.
7. **Newsletters/podcasts in the niche** — pitch the post-mortems as guest
   data pieces once 3–4 exist.

**Explicitly NOT doing** (speed discipline): paid ads, SEO content farming,
conference booths, cold outbound to venues before the Calibration Page,
a blog about "the future of prediction markets," logo/brand work beyond a
wordmark, and any activity that requires being pseudonymous-drama-adjacent.
Neutral referees don't do beef.

## 5. The 90-day sequence (marketing interleaved with TODO.md build order)

**Weeks 1–2 — Foundation (private).** Full backfill + labels (TODO P0).
Register domain, wordmark, X handle. Write 2 post-mortems from escalated
2025–26 disputes as drafts. Nothing public yet — but hash and timestamp the
dataset build now; the receipts start today even if publishing waits.

**Weeks 3–4 — Soft launch (credibility seeding).** Publish post-mortem #1
and #2 + the dataset methodology write-up. HN + X + UMA Discourse. Goal is
not followers; it's 20 of the *right* people (known traders, UMA voters, MM
engineers) following. Start the daily automated Dispute Watchlist.

**Weeks 5–8 — The record accrues.** Watchlist daily; post-mortems weekly;
first pre-resolution calls on live contested markets (hashed). Ship the
linter-only v0 score publicly with honest framing ("rule-based, calibration
pending"). DM the first 30 bot operators with free API keys — their usage is
the seed-round metric and their feedback is the roadmap.

**Weeks 9–12 — Calibration Page live (the launch that counts).** Publish
scores + realized outcomes + methodology + time split. File the CFTC comment
citing the dataset. Pitch 2–3 niche newsletters the launch. From this moment
— and per the non-negotiables, *only* from this moment — selling is allowed.

**Gate:** if the calibration numbers are bad at week 12, we still publish
them (the brand *is* the honesty), we just delay pricing until a v2 score.
What we never do is quietly retrain and pretend. Nothing is sold before the
page is live, and nothing dishonest is sold after.

## 6. Business model & pricing ladder (sequenced, all post-calibration)

**Phase A — Free (months 1–3).** Everything public. The product being sold
is trust; the currency collected is usage and the track record.

**Phase B — API subscriptions (months 3–6).** Meter the existing free API.
- Free tier: delayed scores, rate-limited (keeps the funnel and the citations).
- Bot/trader tier: real-time scores + webhooks, ~$99/mo (hypothesis).
- MM/desk tier: bulk + SLA + historical exports, ~$500–1,500/mo (hypothesis).
- Pricing anchor in all sales conversations: a single mislabeled resolution
  costs a serious position holder more than a year of the subscription. Sell
  it as insurance against wording risk, not as data.
- Target: 10–20 paying bots/desks ≈ €2–10k MRR. Small, but it's *evidence*,
  and evidence is what months 3–6 are for.

**Phase C — Evidence bundles (months 6–12).** Per-case: when a market is
contested, large holders buy the assembled evidence package (timestamped
sources, clause mapping, precedents, multi-model adjudication + dissent).
Price per case ~$500–5,000 scaled to value at stake (hypothesis). This
monetizes exactly the moments when our public content is already the center
of attention — the post-mortem engine doubles as the storefront.

**Phase D — Venue contracts (months 9–18).** The listing gate (score every
draft market pre-listing) + escalation desk + precedent-corpus license.
Five-to-six-figure annual contracts; one regulated venue is the 12–18 month
goal and the seed-round centerpiece. Venues buy after the Calibration Page
has months of history and their own traders already cite us at them — which
is why Phases A–C are the venue sales strategy.

**Deliberately not in the model:** tokens, market operation, custody, revenue
from taking sides. Refuse fast; it compounds the only moat we have.

## 7. Partnerships & the endgame

- **UMA:** the delicate one — partner, data source, and long-term disruptee.
  Posture: make UMA voters demonstrably better (free bundles for active DVM
  votes?), never attack the oracle publicly. The 3–5yr thesis is they hand us
  the subjective tail; that deal is won by being useful for two years.
- **Chainlink:** their "Prediction Market Oracle" wants the objective tail;
  we want the subjective one. Complementary — a joint reference architecture
  is cheap and legitimizing. Explore after calibration launch.
- **Venues:** no partnership motion before the page. After it: the pitch is
  regulatory ("show the CFTC an independent, audited resolution process"),
  not technical.
- **Acquirers (per PLAN):** oracle network, venue, or exchange-data company.
  Everything above — neutrality, public record, corpus — is what makes those
  three bid against each other. Fundraising narrative for the seed (~month
  6–9): "the calibration record + N paying desks + one venue conversation;
  we are becoming the Moody's of market wording."

## 8. Metrics that matter (per phase, in order)

1. Weeks 1–12: dataset completeness; post-mortems published; **days of
   uninterrupted track record** (the north star); followers *of the right
   200 people*; Watchlist opens.
2. Months 3–6: free API keys active weekly; paid conversions; citations
   (newsletters, papers, docket); pre-resolution call accuracy vs. baseline.
3. Months 6–18: MRR; bundles sold per dispute season; venue meetings sourced
   *inbound*; % of major disputes where our bundle is referenced publicly.

Vanity metrics we ignore: total followers, site traffic, Discord size.

## 9. Strategy risks & counters

- **We're wrong publicly, early.** Certain. Counter: confidence intervals on
  everything, a public errata log, and framing every call as recommendation +
  evidence. The errata log is a feature — no competitor with a legal
  department will dare copy it.
- **A venue builds in-house.** Expected. Counter: speed (this document), the
  cross-venue record they can't produce, and being cheap enough to license.
- **ToS/data-access retaliation** (Gamma API cut off). Counter: on-chain data
  is primary by design (PLAN §7); the venue API is convenience. Stay
  scrupulously polite on rate limits; never republish their raw data wholesale.
- **Drama-account gravity.** The niche rewards outrage; the brand requires
  boring. Counter: the "render, don't write" rule — if a script didn't
  produce it, it doesn't ship.
- **Solo-founder bandwidth.** The real constraint. Counter: everything in §3
  is automated output of the existing pipeline; the co-founder hire at month
  3 should own either distribution or venue sales — whichever the founder is
  worse at — not more engineering.
